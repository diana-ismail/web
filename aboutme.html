About Me

I am Diana Ismail, an astrophysicist; currently a postdoctoral researcher at the Observatory of Strabourg in France. I study the cold interstellar medium of distant galaxies to understand how they evolve across cosmic time. Using large telescopes such as NOEMA and ALMA, I focus on some of the dustiest and most intensely star-forming galaxies in the Universe, which could potentially be the progenitors of today's most massive galaxies.

What fascinates me most about astronomy is that the farther we look into space, the farther back in time we travel. The galaxies I study take us 10–12 billion years into the past, offering a glimpse into the young Universe.

I completed my PhD in November 2023 at the Laboratoire d'Astrophysique de Marseille in southern France, under the supervision of Véronique Buat and Alexandre Beelen. During my PhD, I joined the z-GAL NOEMA Large Program, where I played a key role in the data analysis and leading one of the program's main publications. I am still in close collaboration with the team and conducting further research on z-GAL galaxies. Before that, I completed my master's degree in Astrophysics back in Lebanon (2016-2019), in a joint program between Notre Dame University and Université de Saint Joseph, and a bachelor's degree in Physics (2012-2015) at the Lebanese University.
